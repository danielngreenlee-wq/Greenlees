The truck Step 1

it’s basically a 1960s diner on wheels, with chrome, a checkerboard stripe and a striped awning, serving diner food with an irish twist. it’s named after me (Danny Greenlee).

Logo: the shamrock and maple leaf are the truck’s roots, the sun is for energy, and the green meadow is there because greenlee literally means green meadow.

Slogan: sizzling is the sound, salty is the taste, and greens is what you see (and also my name lol).

The meal Step 2

  • Wagyu Chopped Greens: seared wagyu chopped over kale, cabbage, carrots and apple with whole wheat croutons and flaky salt
  • Sweet & Sour Irish Buffalo Potatoes: roasted potatoes in honey buffalo sauce with a yogurt dip
  • Emerald Twist: fizzy orange cream with a root beer twist (orange juice, sparkling water, milk float)

Why it’s craveable: it’s just really good. hot steak on cold crunchy greens, and it’s salty, sweet, sour and a little spicy all at once.

MyPlate + macros Step 2

veggies 40% (greens + potatoes), grains 30% (whole wheat croutons), protein 20% (wagyu), fruit 10% (apple + OJ), and dairy in the cup.

the whole meal is 820 calories with 47% carbs, 19% protein, 34% fat, and all three are inside the recommended ranges.

Good ingredients Step 2

  • complex carbs: potatoes (root veggie) and 100% whole wheat croutons, with 13 g of fiber
  • healthy fats: olive oil and pumpkin seeds, and we roast everything so nothing’s fried
  • real food only: just 4 g of added sugar in the whole meal (one teaspoon of honey)

Vitamins + minerals Step 2

  • fat-soluble: vitamin K, 226% of your day, from the kale and cabbage
  • water-soluble: vitamin C at 169% from the OJ and kale, plus B12 at 113% from the wagyu
  • mineral: potassium, 50% of your day, mostly from the potatoes

Food safety Step 3

145°F  the steak gets cooked to 145°F, then rests 3 minutes before we chop it.

  • clean: wash hands for 20 seconds and sanitize every board and counter
  • separate: red board for raw beef, green board for veggies, and they never touch
  • cook: a food thermometer goes in the thickest part of every single steak

sell by vs use by: sell by is for the store (how long to keep it on the shelf), and use by is the last day to use it at its best. anything past use by gets tossed.
